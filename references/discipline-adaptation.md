# Discipline adaptation

Use only relevant entries. These are selection criteria, not a requirement to add every listed topic.

## Mathematics

- Knowledge: definitions, theorems, corollaries, formulas, quantifiers, and counterexamples.
- Methods: transformations, inequalities, error estimates, induction, contradiction, case analysis, and convergence arguments actually present in the supplied scope.
- Retain conditions such as nonzero denominators, sign restrictions, and pointwise versus uniform control.
- Link estimates to their target: a constant error bound may establish boundedness but does not establish that the error tends to zero.
- A fixed-point equation supplies candidate limits; it does not by itself prove convergence.
- For arbitrary tail lengths, a convergence cutoff must not depend on the tail length.
- These examples illustrate precision; do not insert them into unrelated chapters.

## Physics, circuits, and engineering

- Knowledge: models, laws, constitutive relations, assumptions, approximations, units, and boundary or initial conditions.
- Methods: selecting system boundaries, diagrams, coordinates or reference directions, constructing governing equations, applying constraints, and checking limiting cases.
- Separate a physical law from a model approximation and a special-case formula.
- Preserve current/voltage directions, sign conventions, and peak-versus-RMS quantities when relevant.
- Check dimensions and units, without treating dimensional consistency as proof.
- Map methods to their role: model choice, equation construction, simplification, or validation.

## Programming and computer science

- Knowledge: language constructs, program behavior, data structures, invariants, algorithms, and complexity.
- Methods: decomposition, state representation, algorithm selection, tracing, boundary analysis, and debugging patterns.
- Distinguish syntax from algorithms and working examples from correctness arguments.
- Respect required language/version, allowed techniques, input/output conventions, and learner level.
- Check empty inputs, bounds, overflow, conversions, undefined behavior, termination, and complexity when applicable.
- Label results as executed only if code was run; otherwise state that it was analyzed.
- Do not include full assignment programs when only methods and summaries were requested.

## Chemistry and other sciences

- Knowledge: definitions, models, laws, equations, assumptions, conventions, and applicable regimes.
- Methods: conservation balances, proportional reasoning, model selection, mechanism comparison, and data interpretation.
- Preserve temperature, pressure, concentration, phase, or idealization conditions where relevant.
- Distinguish observations from explanatory models and claims requiring additional evidence.

## Other subjects, when explicitly requested

The knowledge–method–problem structure can extend beyond STEM.

- Language learning: grammatical structures, collocations, discourse logic, question-type signals, response strategies, and typical errors.
- Reading or theory courses: concepts, arguments, evidence, analytical frameworks, and applications to supplied questions.
- Do not invent theorem-like certainty for interpretive judgments; identify supported alternatives.

## Mixed or prerequisite-heavy material

- Preserve the requested course boundary.
- Add only the minimum prerequisite needed for an in-scope method.
- Label prerequisites and extensions separately.
- If a technique comes from a later chapter, identify that and provide an in-scope route when possible.
